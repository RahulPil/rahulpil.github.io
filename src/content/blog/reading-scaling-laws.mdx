I've been working through the scaling laws literature on and off for the last
few weeks, and I want to write down what's stuck before I forget. The point
isn't a survey — there are good ones already — but to record what feels
load-bearing to me now, so I can compare against what feels load-bearing six
months from now.

The thing I keep underestimating is how much of the story is about *what you
measure*. Loss as a function of compute looks clean. The same loss as a function
of any downstream metric does not. A lot of arguments I've read are really
arguments about which axis to plot on, dressed up as arguments about the world.

A short list of things I still don't have good intuition for:

- Why the exponents are so similar across modalities, and whether that's a fact
  about data or a fact about architectures.
- How much of the apparent smoothness of scaling curves is averaging across
  things that, individually, are not smooth.
- What "emergence" means once you take measurement-axis effects seriously.

> The best papers in this area feel less like answers and more like a careful
> accounting of what we don't yet have the tools to ask.

I'll come back to this when I've read more. For now, the takeaway is mostly
methodological: be suspicious of plots, and especially suspicious of the
plots that confirm a story you already believed.
