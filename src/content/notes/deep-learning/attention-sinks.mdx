A recurring observation in trained transformers: a small number of tokens —
often the very first ones in the sequence — end up receiving a
disproportionate fraction of the attention mass across many heads and layers,
even when those tokens carry essentially no semantic content.

The intuitive story I find useful: softmax forces every head to put its
probability mass *somewhere*. When a head has nothing in particular to attend
to on a given step, it dumps the mass onto a fixed "parking spot" rather than
smearing it across the sequence (which would inject noise). Early tokens get
the job because they're visible from every position under causal masking.

The practical consequence is the bit that surprised me: removing these tokens
during inference — say, by truncating long contexts naively — can degrade
quality much more than the tokens' content would suggest. They're load-bearing
*as positions*, not as words.
