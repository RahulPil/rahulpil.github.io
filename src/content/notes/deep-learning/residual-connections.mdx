The residual block from [He et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385) is the deceptively
simple

$$
y = x + f(x).
$$

Stack a thousand of them and the network still trains. Stack a thousand of $y = f(x)$ and it
does not. The standard textbook story is "gradients flow through the identity," which is
true but glosses over *why* the identity is special.

## Gradient view

Let $L$ be the loss and consider a deep stack $x_{l+1} = x_l + f_l(x_l)$. By the chain rule,

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_l} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{L}} \cdot \prod_{i=l}^{L-1} \left( I + \frac{\partial f_i(x_i)}{\partial x_i} \right).
$$

The product is not over Jacobians of $f$; it is over $I + \nabla f$. As long as $\nabla f$
stays small near initialization (which weight init schemes ensure), the product stays close
to the identity and gradient magnitudes are preserved.

Without the residual,

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_l} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{L}} \cdot \prod_{i=l}^{L-1} \frac{\partial f_i(x_i)}{\partial x_i},
$$

and any consistent skew in the spectrum of $\nabla f$ — even slightly less than 1 on
average — compounds into vanishing gradients.

## Loss landscape view

[Li et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09913) visualized loss surfaces of deep networks
with and without skip connections. Without: a jagged terrain pocked with sharp minima.
With: a smooth, convex-ish basin. The residual connections are essentially a *prior toward
identity* — they make "do nothing" the network's default behavior, and each layer only has
to learn what to add on top.

## A useful intuition

A residual network is a **shallow ensemble in disguise**.
[Veit et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06431) showed that the effective paths through
a ResNet are not the full depth — they form a distribution over path lengths, peaked at a
much shorter depth than the architecture suggests. The model behaves more like an ensemble
of moderately deep networks than one very deep one.

Which is to say: residual connections didn't make deep networks work. They made
*moderately-deep ensembles* work, and we labeled the result depth.
