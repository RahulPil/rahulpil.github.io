A reframing I keep returning to: a residual block computes $x \mapsto x + f(x)$,
which means the *default* behavior of every layer is to leave the
representation alone. A layer only contributes when $f(x)$ is meaningfully
non-zero. Training, in this view, is largely about deciding which layers
should bother to do anything at a given depth.

This frames a lot of small empirical observations cleanly: many layers in a
trained transformer have near-identity behavior on many inputs; pruning some
of them barely moves the loss; the "residual stream" picture in mechanistic
interpretability work treats each layer as writing a small additive update
into a shared workspace. All of these are the same idea seen from different
angles.

The reason I like the framing is that it makes the surprising thing about deep
networks not their depth but their *selective use* of depth.
