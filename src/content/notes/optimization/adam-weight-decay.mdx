Plain Adam with an L2 penalty added to the loss does *not* do what you think
it does. The penalty gets folded into the gradient before Adam's per-parameter
adaptive rescaling, which means parameters with large second-moment estimates
get a smaller effective weight decay than parameters with small ones. The
shrinkage is no longer uniform — it depends on the optimizer's running
statistics, which is exactly the thing you didn't want regularization to
depend on.

AdamW fixes this by applying the decay $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \lambda \theta$
*after* the adaptive update, decoupled from the gradient path entirely. The
result is the simple thing you wanted in the first place: every parameter
shrinks toward zero at the same rate, regardless of how Adam happens to be
rescaling its gradient. Most modern training recipes that say "Adam" really
mean AdamW, and the distinction shows up most visibly with long training runs
and large models.
