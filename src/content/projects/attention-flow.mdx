## Why

Static heatmaps of attention weights are useful but ungenerous to the reader. A 12-layer,
12-head transformer has 144 heatmaps per inference; nobody is going to print and stare at
them. *Attention-flow* was an attempt to make attention behavior something you can poke at
with your hands.

## What

A WebGL canvas that renders attention as light flowing between tokens. Hovering a token
foregrounds all heads that attend to it; clicking a head shows its attention pattern across
the full sequence. The model runs in the browser via ONNX Runtime, so there's no backend —
you can paste a sentence and see how it changes.

The visual metaphor is borrowed from the *streamlines* used in fluid simulation: attention
weight is treated as the magnitude of a flow field, and curves are integrated through it
from each query token. Heads with focused attention produce sharp lines; diffuse heads
produce hazy washes.

## What didn't work

I tried, for a while, to color heads by their function — induction heads in one hue,
positional heads in another, etc. The classification was too brittle. Two heads with
nearly identical patterns on one input would behave entirely differently on the next.
Mechanistic interpretability remains harder than I'd like.
