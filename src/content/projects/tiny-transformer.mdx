## Why

Every ML student has heard *attention is all you need*. Fewer of us have typed every line
of a transformer ourselves, without leaning on a library's pre-built module. The point of
this project was not to produce a competitive model — it was to make sure I understood the
shapes.

## What

A 6-million parameter decoder-only transformer:

- 6 layers, 6 heads, $d_{\text{model}} = 192$
- Rotary positional embeddings
- SwiGLU MLP
- RMSNorm pre-norm
- Trained on **TinyStories** with a context length of 256
- ~85 minutes on a single A100

The full implementation is ~500 lines of PyTorch. No `nn.MultiheadAttention`, no
`nn.TransformerEncoderLayer`. The attention, MLP, positional encoding, and training loop
were all hand-rolled.

## What I learned

The thing nobody tells you is how many silent bugs you can ship. A wrong axis on a softmax
will still train — slowly, and badly, but it will train. The clearest signal during
debugging came from logging the loss curves of *every layer's activations*, not just the
final loss. A misimplemented residual connection looks like a flat training loss; a
misimplemented attention mask looks like a slightly elevated loss that never recovers.

Numerics matter more than they should. Switching from `float32` accumulation in attention
softmax to `bfloat16` cut throughput by 15% but eliminated a quiet instability around step
4k.

## Next

I want to try interpretability tooling on this model — the model is small enough to look
at every head, and the dataset is simple enough that the circuits should be legible.
